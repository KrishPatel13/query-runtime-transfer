# Widened workload check on M1

Generated by `scripts/check_workload.py` from the run of 2026-09-30. Do not edit by hand; run the script again to update it.

This is a sanity check of `queries/tpch_sf1_v2`, not a label collection. Every query was planned with `EXPLAIN (FORMAT JSON)`, and every widened query ran once untimed and then once timed (statement timeout 120 s), timed as `src/collect.py` times a run. Q11 also ran at both ends of its fraction, to measure how much spread its template allows. Method and criteria are in the docstring of `scripts/check_workload.py`; the widened ranges and why they were chosen are in the docstring of `scripts/generate_widened_queries.py`.

## Result

- Plans: 680 of 680 queries plan without error. 420 of 420 standard queries have the same plan as in the pilot (node tree, estimated costs and rows), so the database and its statistics are unchanged.
- Widened queries: 260 of 260 finished. The slowest execution, untimed run included, took 9.0 s (limit 60 s); over the limit: none.
- Spread, slowest over fastest widened query of a template: at least 2 in 12 of 13 templates; below 2: Q11. Not wider than the standard queries' spread: none. Q11's template allows a spread of about 1.44 on M1 (section Q11 below).
- Templates whose widened queries all have the same estimated cost, because PostgreSQL's estimates do not depend on the widened value: Q11, Q18.

## Q11

Q11's parameters are a nation and a fraction, and the fraction only decides how many of the aggregated parts are kept and sorted. Fraction 0 keeps every part (27,365 to 32,899, depending on the nation) and fraction 1 keeps none. The widened fractions keep from 0 to 30,604 parts, so they reach both ends.

Timed at both ends for each of the 25 nations (median of 3 runs after an untimed one), keeping every part instead of none changed the runtime by a median factor of 1.10 (0.97 to 1.43 over the nations). All 50 of these runs lay between 69.1 and 99.7 ms, a spread of 1.44. Any Q11 query runs between the two ends of its nation, so up to measurement noise this is the widest spread the template allows on M1. The 20 widened Q11 queries, timed the same way, ran from 80.9 to 101 ms (spread 1.25). For comparison, the 3 timed runs of one query differed by a median factor of 1.09 (up to 1.41).
